1. Housing Pipeline (Null-Case Bounded-Lag Comparison)

The housing construction pipeline serves as the primary bounded-lag null comparison. Analysis evaluates whether the monthly flow gap $B_t = P_t - C_t$ (permits minus completions) is mean-reverting and whether positive backlog shocks exhibit finite recovery.

Figure 1.1: Historical U.S. Housing Construction Pipeline (PERMIT, HOUST, COMPUTSA, SAAR, 1968–2026).

Key Statistical Properties ($B_t$)

  • Sample Interval: 1968-01-01 to 2026-08-01 (704 months).
  • Stationarity (ADF Test): $t = -7.41$, $p < 10^{-10}$ (Unit root firmly rejected).
  • KPSS Test: $p > 0.10$ (Stationarity cannot be rejected).
  • AR(1) Persistence ($\phi$): $0.52$ (Shock half-life = 1.1 months).
  • Mean Shock Recovery Time: 2.4 months following a +1 SD backlog shock.

Queueing & Drift Diagnostics ($H_t$)

  • Cumulative Flow Proxy ($H_t$): Constructed as $H_t = \sum B_t$.
  • Unit Root Status ($H_t$): $I(1)$ unit root drift due to mechanical cumulative summation.
  • Operational Conclusion: Physical pipeline is bounded; one-period positive differences do not imply instability.
  • Pre/Post-GFC Break: Flow difference remains stationary in both regimes ($p < 10^{-6}$).

3. Anthropic AI Distillation Activity

Reported distillation activity from Anthropic's February 2026 disclosure was analyzed to separate observed extraction-input volume from realized student capability gains.

Methodological Distinction: Reported exchange counts ($15\text{M}$ exchanges) measure input rate ($\lambda_{\text{obs}} = 166,667\text{ exch/day}$), not student capability transfer ($\mu$). The parameter $\mu_{\text{eff}} = \frac{r u v \lambda_{\text{obs}}}{h}$ is evaluated across a sensitivity grid spanning four orders of magnitude.

4. Longevity Biotechnology: Evidence vs. Access Decoupling

The CALERIE trial provides human clinical biomarker evidence of biological aging deceleration (DunedinPACE: $-0.02$, $p=0.008$; PhenoAge: $-0.11\text{ SD}$, $p=0.031$). However, demonstrated lifespan extension remains unresolved.

Figure 4.1: Monthly Intervention Out-of-Pocket Cost vs. Affordability Index ($P^* = \$200$/month).

While generic metformin is cheap ($4.00/mo, Affordability = 0.98$), it lacks longevity approval. Full-body concierge protocols ($1,250.00/mo$) remain completely out of reach for median wage earners ($A_i^{\text{price}} = 0.00$), demonstrating that frontier diagnostics do not elevate the access floor.
