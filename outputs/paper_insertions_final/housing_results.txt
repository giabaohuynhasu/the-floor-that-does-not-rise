The historical housing test evaluates whether the permit-to-completion flow difference is mean-reverting and whether backlog shocks exhibit finite recovery. A one-period positive difference is not treated as evidence of instability. The analysis is conducted on the overlapping historical window (1968–2026, N=685 monthly observations) for which permits, starts, and completions are simultaneously available.

Empirically, the monthly flow difference B_t = P_t - C_t is stationary under Augmented Dickey-Fuller (ADF statistic = -7.41, p < 1e-10) and KPSS tests, with an estimated AR(1) persistence coefficient of phi = 0.52 and a shock half-life of 1.1 months. Positive backlog shocks returning to baseline within an empirical average of 2.4 months confirm that the housing construction pipeline constitutes an operationally bounded system. While the cumulative flow proxy H_t exhibits mechanical unit-root drift due to accumulation, the underlying flow gap remains strictly mean-reverting across both pre-GFC and post-GFC subsamples.
