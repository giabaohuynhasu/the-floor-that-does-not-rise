CALERIE provides human biomarker evidence of altered biological-aging trajectories (e.g., DunedinPACE pace of aging reduction of 2–3%, p = 0.008; PhenoAge deceleration of -0.11 SD, p = 0.031), but not direct evidence of extended lifespan or reduced all-cause mortality. 

Price observations are treated strictly as access proxies across distinct market layers: while generic metformin presents a highly affordable monthly retail cost ($4.00/mo, affordability index = 0.98 at a $200 threshold), it remains unindicated for longevity and lacks phase-3 survival validation. Conversely, off-label rapamycin ($65.00/mo) requires clinical monitoring that creates non-price barriers, and comprehensive multi-omic concierge protocols ($1,250.00/mo) remain strictly unaffordable for the general population (affordability index = 0.00). A cheap molecule can remain outside the validated longevity floor if evidence, indication, monitoring, or provider capacity is binding.
